# Market Research Guide

## Sources

- Freelance marketplaces: Fiverr, Upwork, Freelancer, PeoplePerHour, Truelancer, Freelancer.in
- Agency and directory pages: Clutch, GoodFirms, DesignRush, agency pricing pages
- Communities and discussions: Reddit, Indie Hackers, Stack Exchange, developer Discords/forums
- Public case studies and cost write-ups

Guru has low value now; skip unless relevant.

## Search plan

Generate different queries by feature, stack, category, complexity, page count,
integrations and industry. Example for a SaaS dashboard:

- "SaaS dashboard development cost"
- "Next.js dashboard developer Fiverr"
- "admin dashboard Upwork fixed price budget"
- "subscription SaaS MVP development agency estimate"

Budget: Quick 3-5 searches, Full 8-12. Stop when you have 5-8 relevant
comparables or the searches stop returning new ones. Do not pad with irrelevant
results to hit a number.

## Access fallback

If a marketplace page blocks or needs login:

1. Use the search snippet and cap evidence at E3 or E5.
2. Try another source for the same service type.
3. Mark the page "unavailable" in the table. Never invent its contents.

## Record each comparable

| Platform | URL | Service | Price | Currency | Package | Scope | Delivery | Evidence (E1-E5) | Access date |

Prefer sources from the last 18 months. Mark older ones.

## Normalise and summarise

1. Convert each price to INR using one live rate (source and date recorded).
2. Adjust for scope: price per comparable feature set, not raw listing price.
3. Remove clear outliers (far below or above, wrong scope).
4. Report the **median and interquartile range**, plus min and max.
5. Weight higher-evidence items (E1, E2) more than E3-E5 and say so.

## Regional view

Identify the client's physical location, company registration, target audience
and the market the product will sell in; these can differ. Research the local
freelancer and agency market for that region. Consider company size, industry,
purchasing power and support expectations. Do not assume Indian clients have low
budgets or foreign clients high ones.
