# Negotiation Playbook

Generate replies only when the user asks or the client has pushed back. Keep
each reply short, polite and commercially sensible. Protect the Minimum price;
never reveal it or the buffer.

## First, diagnose
Is the issue **budget** (cannot afford it) or **perceived value** (does not see why it costs this)? Reply differently.

## Rules
1. Explain price by deliverables, not by hours or margin.
2. Offer scope-based alternatives before any discount.
3. No arbitrary discounts. Any reduction must remove real work (show hours saved).
4. New features mean a change request with its own estimate.
5. Never accept unlimited requirements or unlimited revisions on a fixed price.

## Reply patterns

**Price too high (value gap)**
"Thanks for the feedback. The price covers [deliverables], testing, deployment and [warranty]. If you want, I can share a leaner version without [features] at [price]."

**Limited budget**
"Happy to work within a budget. At [budget] I can deliver [core scope]. [Deferred features] can follow in phase 2."

**Wants extra features**
"Good idea. That is outside the agreed scope, so I'll send a separate estimate (about [time] and [price]) for it."

**Free revisions**
"Two revision rounds are included in the price. Anything beyond that is billed at [rate or fixed fee] so timelines stay predictable."

**Compares with Fiverr or cheap quotes**
"Marketplace listings usually show a starting price for a limited package. Ours covers [full scope, support, handover]. If you share the other quote, I can compare what is included."

**Faster delivery**
"I can compress delivery to [date] by [reduced scope / priority fee of x%]. A rush also depends on [content/approvals] arriving on time."

**Wants a discount**
"I can offer [small concession tied to something real: faster payment, a 100% advance, scope trim, or a case-study permission]."

**Post-launch support**
"The price includes [15-30] days of bug-fix warranty. Ongoing support is available as a monthly plan at [price] covering [hours/response time]."

## Walk-away rule
If the client insists on scope or price below Minimum, say so politely and decline or propose phase 1 only.
